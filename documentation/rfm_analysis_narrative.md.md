# The Core Finding

This analysis set out to answer one question:
***Are we retaining the customers we already paid
to acquire?***

The answer is **NO**.

***80% of customers — 74,838 out of 93,358 — never
returned after their first purchase. Of those,
98% placed exactly one order and disappeared
the same day.***

**This is not a churn problem. This is a
single-purchase problem**.

# The Story the Data Tells

| **#** | Finding | What it Means in Plain English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 80.16% churn rate | 8 out of every 10 customers never came back |
| 2 | 98% of churned customers are single-purchase buyers | Most customers try the platform once and never return — not because they had a bad experience over time, but because nothing brought them back |
| 3 | Average revenue per customer declining from 184 to 162 | Newer customers are spending less — the business is growing in volume but shrinking in value per customer |
| 4 | Retention drops to under 1% by month 1 for most cohorts | The critical window to win a customer back is the first 30 days after their first purchase |

# Business Recommendations

| **#** | Recommendation | Based On |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Launch a 30-day post-purchase email sequence for all new customers | 98% of churn happens after a single purchase — the first 30 days are the critical re-engagement window |
| 2 | Introduce a loyalty program or second-purchase discount | Average revenue per customer is declining — incentivizing repeat purchases increases both retention and CLV |
| 3 | Investigate earliest cohorts (Oct 2016) for retention tactics | Oct 2016 cohort shows slightly higher avg revenue (184) — understand what was different about early customers |
| 4 | Shift marketing budget from pure acquisition to retention | The critical window to win a customer back is the first 30 days after their first purchase |
